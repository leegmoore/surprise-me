# Understanding Alex

**A deep dive into who we're building for.**

---

## Who Alex Is

**Alex, 28, UX Designer in Austin**

But honestly, Alex could be:
- A remote software engineer in Portland, 31
- A grad student in Chicago, 24
- A freelance writer in a small town in Ohio, 35
- A nurse working 3x12 shifts in Phoenix, 29

The demographics don't matter. The *situation* does.

---

## What Unites All Alexes

### They're not in crisis.

Alex isn't depressed. Isn't burnt out (not severely, anyway). Isn't looking for therapy or self-help. They're just... *fine*. And "fine" has started to feel like a rut.

### They have autonomy but not direction.

Alex has more control over their time than they did at 22. But that freedom came without instructions. "Do whatever you want" is paralyzing when you don't know what you want.

### They're aware of the scroll trap.

Alex knows that opening TikTok/Instagram/Reddit for "just a sec" leads to 45 minutes of nothing. They don't love this about themselves. But the alternative requires *deciding something*, and that's exhausting.

### They romanticize spontaneity but don't practice it.

Alex watches travel vlogs. Saves "100 things to do in your city" articles. Thinks "I should be more adventurous." But Saturday comes and they're on the couch again, vaguely disappointed in themselves.

### They're not lonely, but they're a little isolated.

Alex has friends. A partner, maybe. But adult life has this way of making everything scheduled. They miss the unplanned hangouts of college. The randomness.

---

## The Moments We're Designing For

These are the specific situations when Alex would reach for Nudge:

---

### Moment 1: "The Sunday Scaries Gap"

**The situation:**
It's Sunday, 3pm. Alex finished their errands. Too early to make dinner. Too late to start anything "big." They pick up their phone, scroll for 20 minutes, feel gross, put it down. Repeat.

**What's happening internally:**
- Low-grade restlessness
- Guilt about "wasting the day"
- Decision fatigue (too many options = no options)
- A vague sense that they *should* be doing something

**How Nudge helps:**
Nudge removes the decision. It says: "Here's a thing. It takes 15 minutes. You can do it right now. It's slightly interesting." The activation energy drops from "figure out what to do" to "just do this one thing."

**Example nudge for this moment:**
> "Walk to the nearest tree. Stand under it for 2 minutes. Look up."
>
> *⏱ 10 min | 🌡 Low energy | 📍 Just outside*

**What success looks like:**
Alex comes back inside feeling slightly more alive. Not transformed. Just... less stagnant. They think, "Huh, that was nice. I wouldn't have done that on my own."

---

### Moment 2: "The Post-Work Void"

**The situation:**
It's 6:15pm on a Tuesday. Alex closed their laptop. They're not tired enough to veg out, not energized enough to go to the gym. The evening stretches ahead, formless.

**What's happening internally:**
- Residual work brain (hard to switch modes)
- A desire to do something that isn't productive but isn't passive
- Low social energy (don't want to make plans, but also... bored?)

**How Nudge helps:**
Nudge offers a small, self-contained activity that marks the transition from "work mode" to "life mode." It's a ritual that isn't meditation or exercise — it's just *a thing to do*.

**Example nudge for this moment:**
> "Make a list of 5 sounds you can hear right now. Pick your favorite."
>
> *⏱ 3 min | 🌡 Low energy | 📍 Right here*

Or if they want something more active:
> "Go to a store you've never been in. Buy nothing. Leave."
>
> *⏱ 20 min | 🌡 Medium energy | 📍 Nearby*

**What success looks like:**
Alex does the nudge. It takes 8 minutes. They feel like they "did something" without it being A Whole Thing. The evening opens up a little.

---

### Moment 3: "The New City Stranger"

**The situation:**
Alex moved to a new city 6 months ago. They know their apartment, their office, the coffee shop on the corner. That's it. The city feels huge and impersonal. They keep meaning to "explore" but don't know where to start.

**What's happening internally:**
- Overwhelm (too many options, no local knowledge)
- Low-key loneliness
- Desire to feel like they *live here* not just *reside here*
- Fear of looking like a tourist/outsider

**How Nudge helps:**
Nudge gives specific, achievable exploration prompts that don't require research or planning. It makes "exploring" feel like a game instead of a project.

**Example nudge for this moment:**
> "Find a menu in a restaurant window. Pick what you'd order. Leave without going in."
>
> *⏱ 15 min | 🌡 Medium energy | 📍 Walking distance*

Or:
> "Ride public transit one stop past where you need to go. Walk back."
>
> *⏱ 30 min | 🌡 Medium energy | 📍 Requires transit*

**What success looks like:**
Alex discovers a neighborhood they didn't know existed. Or finds a weird statue. Or just *walks* somewhere new. The city shrinks a little. Feels more like theirs.

---

### Moment 4: "The Stuck Creative"

**The situation:**
Alex has a creative hobby — writing, drawing, photography, whatever. They haven't touched it in weeks. Every time they think about it, they feel this wall of "I don't have ideas" or "I'm not in the mood."

**What's happening internally:**
- Perfectionism paralysis ("if I can't do it well, why start")
- Blank page intimidation
- Guilt about neglecting something they care about
- A missing sense of permission to make something bad

**How Nudge helps:**
Nudge gives a creative constraint. Constraints are freedom. Instead of "make something," it's "make *this specific thing* in *this specific way*." The pressure dissolves because you're just following instructions.

**Example nudge for this moment:**
> "Draw something in your kitchen using only straight lines. 3 minutes max. It should look bad."
>
> *⏱ 3 min | 🌡 Low energy | 📍 Right here*

Or:
> "Write 5 fake review titles for a product that doesn't exist."
>
> *⏱ 5 min | 🌡 Low energy | 📍 Right here*

**What success looks like:**
Alex makes something dumb. Laughs at it. The muscle remembers what it's like to create without stakes. Maybe they keep going. Maybe they don't. Either way, the wall cracked a little.

---

### Moment 5: "The Comfortable Rut"

**The situation:**
Alex's life is *fine*. Good, even. But every week feels the same. Same restaurants. Same routes. Same routines. They're not unhappy — just... unurprised. By anything. Ever.

**What's happening internally:**
- Creeping sameness
- A worry that life is passing by in a blur
- Nostalgia for when things felt more *vivid*
- Low-key boredom they don't fully admit to

**How Nudge helps:**
Nudge injects micro-novelty. It doesn't upend their life. It just adds small variations that compound into a more textured existence. Over time, Alex has stories. Memories. "Remember that time I..."

**Example nudge for this moment:**
> "Take a different route home. Notice one thing you've never seen before."
>
> *⏱ +10 min to your commute | 🌡 Low energy | 📍 On your way*

Or:
> "Eat a meal in a room you don't usually eat in."
>
> *⏱ Your usual meal time | 🌡 Low energy | 📍 Your home*

**What success looks like:**
Alex's week has one more thing in it. One small story. "I took a different route yesterday and found this tiny park." That's it. That's everything.

## The Emotional Journey

**Before Nudge:**
"I have time but no idea what to do with it. I'll just scroll until I figure it out. Ugh, it's been 40 minutes."

**Getting a Nudge:**
"Huh, that's oddly specific. I guess I could do that? It's just 10 minutes..."

**During the Nudge:**
"This is kind of silly. But also... kind of nice? I'm noticing things."

**After the Nudge:**
"That was small but it was *something*. I feel slightly more present. Slightly more alive. I'd do that again."

**Over time:**
"Nudge gave me a bunch of tiny adventures I wouldn't have had otherwise. None of them were life-changing. All of them mattered a little."

---

## What Nudge Is NOT For

- Productivity (we're not optimizing, we're living)
- Self-improvement (no "drink more water" energy)
- Replacing real adventure (we're a side dish, not the main course)
- Depressed/anxious people in crisis (we're not therapy)
- People who are already great at spontaneity (they don't need us)

---

## The Core Insight

**Alex doesn't lack time. Alex lacks activation energy.**

The gap between "I could do something" and "I'm doing something" is enormous when you have to figure out *what*. Nudge is a bridge across that gap. We hand Alex a specific, small, slightly weird thing to do — and that's often all they need.
