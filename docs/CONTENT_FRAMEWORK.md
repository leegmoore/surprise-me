# Nudge Content Framework

**The rules for writing nudges that actually work.**

---

## The Core Principle

A nudge should feel like a dare from a slightly weird friend. Not a command. Not homework. Not self-help. Just: "Hey, try this. See what happens."

---

## The 10 Qualities of a Good Nudge

### 1. Specific
Not "go for a walk" — that's advice. A nudge is: "Walk until you find a door you've never noticed before."

Specificity removes decision-making. The person knows exactly what to do.

### 2. Immediate
Can start right now. No prep. No "first, get..." No prerequisites.

If it requires buying something, planning something, or waiting for something — it's not a nudge.

### 3. Time-bounded
Has a natural end. "Walk until you find X" ends when you find X. "Spend 5 minutes on Y" ends in 5 minutes.

Open-ended prompts feel like obligations. Nudges feel completable.

### 4. Slightly Weird
Not obvious. If it's something Alex would have thought of on their own, it's not interesting enough.

The weirdness is a feature. It makes the mundane feel like an adventure.

### 5. Physical or Sensory
Gets you out of your head and into your body or environment. Look, listen, touch, move.

Not introspective. Not journaling. Not meditation. Those are fine, but they're not nudges.

### 6. Zero Equipment
Just you. Maybe your phone (for a timer, or to take a photo). Nothing you'd need to go get.

### 7. Solo-able
No other people required. Can be done alone, right now.

(Social nudges exist but they're a different category with different rules.)

### 8. Failure-proof
There's no wrong way to do it. No "you should have found something better" energy.

If the nudge is "find the oldest thing in the room" — whatever they find IS the oldest thing. Done.

### 9. Playful, Not Earnest
No self-improvement energy. No "this will help you be more mindful." No wellness vibes.

Tone is: curious, slightly mischievous, inviting. Not: wise, instructive, healing.

### 10. Leaves a Memory
Specific enough that they'll remember doing it. "That time I counted all the circles in my kitchen."

Vague activities blur together. Specific ones stick.

## The Anti-Patterns (What to Avoid)

### Vague
❌ "Notice something beautiful today."
✓ "Find the ugliest thing within 20 feet. Stare at it for 30 seconds."

### Preachy
❌ "Practice gratitude by listing three things you're thankful for."
✓ "Pick an object you use every day. Imagine the person who manufactured it."

### Requires Prep
❌ "Go buy a small plant and find a place for it."
✓ "Water something. If nothing needs water, water something anyway."

### Too Ambitious
❌ "Spend an hour exploring a neighborhood you've never been to."
✓ "Go outside. Turn left. Walk for exactly 3 minutes. Turn around."

### Too Boring
❌ "Take a short walk to clear your head."
✓ "Walk until you hear a sound you can't identify. Stand there for a minute."

### Socially Risky
❌ "Ask a stranger for a recommendation."
✓ "Eavesdrop on a conversation for 30 seconds. Make up what they're really talking about."

### Weather/Location Dependent
❌ "Watch the sunset."
✓ "Find the warmest spot in your current space. Sit there."

### Has a Right Answer
❌ "Find the most interesting architecture nearby."
✓ "Find a building you've never looked at closely. Count its windows."

## Nudge Structure

Every nudge has:

1. **The action** (verb first, clear and concrete)
2. **The constraint** (what makes it specific)
3. **Optional: the twist** (what makes it interesting)

**Examples:**

| Action | Constraint | Twist |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Find | the oldest thing in the room | Imagine who touched it first |
| Walk | for exactly 3 minutes | Then turn around |
| Listen | until you hear 5 different sounds | Name them out loud |
| Touch | the coldest surface nearby | Then the warmest |

---

## Categories

For v1, all nudges will be: **Solo, Physical/Observational, <15 min, No Equipment**

Later, we might add:
- Social (involves another person)
- Creative (make something)
- Timed (specific duration challenge)
- Night (after dark specific)

But for now: one type, done well.

---

## Tone Guide

**Voice:** Curious friend, not life coach.

**Length:** 1-2 sentences max. Shorter is better.

**No:**
- Exclamation points
- "Try to..."
- "Maybe you could..."
- "This might help you..."
- Emojis
- Wellness language ("mindful," "present," "gratitude")

**Yes:**
- Direct imperatives ("Find," "Go," "Stand")
- Specific numbers ("3 minutes," "5 things," "20 feet")
- Concrete nouns ("door," "sound," "window")
- Slight mystery ("see what happens")

---

## Quality Test

Before adding a nudge, ask:

1. Can I start this in the next 30 seconds?
2. Will I know when I'm done?
3. Is it specific enough that two people would do it similarly?
4. Is it weird enough that I wouldn't have thought of it myself?
5. Could I fail at this? (If yes, rewrite it.)
6. Does it sound like homework? (If yes, rewrite it.)
7. Will I remember doing it tomorrow?

If any answer is wrong, the nudge isn't ready.
